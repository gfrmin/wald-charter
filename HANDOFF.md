## 1. What wald is, and why it exists

**One question:** what is the smallest thing you can write down such that everything an agent does follows from it, and anything it should not do is unsayable? wald's answer is Bayesian decision theory as law: a World declares a finite state space, a prior, acts with utilities, observations with kernels and prices; the kernel conditions and maximises expected utility, exactly, in rationals; nothing else may choose.

**Lineage.** wald is the third attempt at one vision. `gfrmin/credence` (Julia, aborted) had the right core and shipped apps, but its builder held the pen on the reasoner, CI never ran the engine's tests, arithmetic was Float64, and its router learned a reliability with no ground truth. `gfrmin/proplang` (Haskell, research) is the cornered, self-referential design and the target of the later stages. wald's one asset neither had is **a signed law, an external kit that judges any implementation, and a builder that cannot touch either.** Protect that above everything.

**Stages, one vision** (CHARTER v0.2 §1 holds the map): 0 the decision is auditable (v0, v0.1); 1 the World learns its instruments (v0.2); 2 hypotheses generated by a grammar, prior as description length (proplang's model fragment); 3 the verbs sayable inside a pack — reflexivity, the gap between a calculator and an agent; 4 utility as a latent. Each stage must be useful alone and forced by a measurement (§8 of v0: no amendment without one).

## 3. The discipline — state once, trace everything, attack last

Adopted on 2026-09-24, after two v0.2 pages took fifteen and eight drafts. Most of those drafts went to finding problems a machine could have found: the page and the reference drifting apart, a fix not carried to a second place the rule was stated, a rule beaten by re-spelling a World, a host language's edge case. So:

- **Every rule is stated once, with an ID** (`V2.k` for SURFACE v0.2; other signed pages' rules cited as `C2.S12`, `V0.reads`). Every other section cites IDs; derived sections — the refusal index, the traceability table — are generated by `laws/page_check.py --render`, never written by hand.
- **Every refusal in a reference names its rule**: `[V2.k]` in the message in `surface_check.py`, `refused(NAME, "C2.X")` in `counts_check.py`. Every poison declares `# expect: NAME` and `# rule: ID`; a lawful pack declares `# exercises: ID, ...`. The declaration states *intent*: never bootstrap it from what the code does without reading every line.
- **`laws/gate.py` must pass before any attack, review or signing.** It runs the references, `page_check.py` (each rule stated once; every refusal site traced to a rule; every (rule, name) a poison; every poison dying by its declared rule; every rule tested; generated sections current), `invariance_check.py` (renaming, padding with a one-valued component either way, and splitting a Global into twins must change no verdict, act, value or belief), `encoding_check.py` (a second encoder written from the page's byte rules alone, against the reference on fuzzed names), and the kit's stand-ins. On its first run it found a disagreement three attack sessions had missed.
- **A rule draws its meaning only from signed pages.** An erratum records a correction; it binds nothing until a signed page states the rule in full (page_check T6).
- **Reviews and attacks are the last resort.** When one finds something, ask first which gate check should have found it, and add that check. Two such checks so far: names drawn from the characters that meet the page's own syntax (quotes, backslashes, control characters, `=`, `:`, non-ASCII, astral), because a renaming that only appends a letter never reaches the syntax; and **machine-read tables** — where a rule is a mapping, as V2.13's escapes are, state it as a table the gate parses, so the check is built from the page and not from the author's reading of it.
- **The typed data model** (`laws/model.py`) states every shape once and, since kit v0.13, the values too (`check_values`: rows are distributions, tables keyed by exactly Ω, prices at least 0, a catch-all gives every outcome mass). `model_check.py` runs it on every World the references build and on every World the reference accepts from a mutated pack. `model_check.py` also runs mypy on `model.py` whenever mypy is installed (it is on steel, and was not on the thinkpad), and says in its summary line when it is not, or when `WALD_TYPECHECK=0` turned it off. The check is plain mypy, not `--strict`: strict fails on 41 missing annotations on helpers (measured 2026-09-30, mypy 1.20.0), none a shape error, and nothing yet forces that work.
- **Kit v0.13's five gate checks**, each built from a class of bug the builder found in brief 008, and each shown failing on the reference before it was fixed (commit `7329b76`):
  - `mutation_check.py`: every mutation of every corpus pack is a World or a named refusal;
  - `model_check.py`'s values;
  - `realise_check.py`: realisability is exactly what v0's loop can write;
  - `roundtrip_check.py`: what the reference plate writes, the reference reads back;
  - two invariances in `invariance_check.py`: a component value the prior does not name, and the text around names.

  The gate now has thirteen checks and runs them at once, in about two minutes.
- **A signed page's generated sections are as of signing.** Once the corpus grows past a page's signing, `page_check.py` asks only that nothing the page lists has gone, and keeps the current rendering in `laws/generated/`.

## 4. The cycle — how every change is made

1. **A measurement forces it.** A consumer's number, a failed attack, a builder's question. Write it as §0 of the page.
2. **Reference first, page second; the gate before anyone else.** The reference checker (`laws/*_check.py`) is written in exact `Fraction`s before or with the page. Every numbered claim in a page is reproduced by the reference; every known bug becomes a **poison** (an implementation or pack that must be refused or must fail a consequence); every appendix is a **frozen vector**. `spec_check.py` is v0, `meta_check.py` v0.1, `counts_check.py` v0.2, `surface_check.py` all of SURFACE.
3. **The owner rules the marks.** Recommend, briefly, with the alternative stated; the owner decides; fill the table with date.
4. **Attack sessions until one finds nothing.** Fresh, isolated, a different model each time. Stop rule: sign when a session finds nothing reproducible in the page's substantive categories; if it finds only wording, fix and sign; otherwise resolve and attack again. Reproduce **every** finding against your own reference before believing or rejecting it; pin each as a vector; log each session in the page's attack log with what it found and how it was resolved.
5. **Sign.** Merge the draft branch, set the status to signed, **delete the branch before tagging** (a branch and a tag of the same name make `git push origin <name>` ambiguous), `git tag -s`, `git tag -v`, push the tag as `refs/tags/<name>`.
6. **Kit bump.** A new judge `laws/kit_<name>.py` (with a `--standin` self-test: the reference passes, a known liar fails), wired into `laws/kit.py`, and its contract as a new section of `laws/INTERFACE.md`. Run the whole kit against the current kernel: it must fail *only* on what the next brief asks for. Tag `kit-vX.Y`.
7. **Lock bump through the door** in `gfrmin/wald`: `cage/charter.lock` gets the kit tag and its SHA; `cage/fetch_charter.sh` checks every signed page against its tag (add each new page); the owner disables the `cage` ruleset, pushes `master`, re-enables it.
8. **Brief.** `briefs/NNN-name.md`: "Done when" (the cage green on a PR from `b/NNN-name`), the owner's numbers (usually none), numbered items that cite the page, what to report in the PR. Say that a performance problem is a stop, not an approximation, and that disagreements go to `QUESTIONS.md`.
9. **Verify the builder's PR** against the kit **exactly as tagged** — rebuild it from the tag, never from your working tree, which will have moved on. Check protected paths are untouched, `cage/lint_imports.py`, the whole kit on an unseen seed, then read the code (no float, no log, no clock; `decide.py` untouched unless the brief says otherwise). Answer every new `QUESTIONS.md` entry: most are defects in *your* reference or kit, and the builder's reading is usually right. Write the PR body; the owner merges.

## 7. What was learned the hard way

**About the law**
- Work from first principles and use the field's vocabulary. Three coinages (Latent, Carry, Cohort) were withdrawn for Global, local, plate. Every noun must pass a deletion audit: remove it and some capability must die.
- A *refusal* rule that reads the World's spelling gets beaten by a re-spelling. S15 went from refusing unidentifiable Globals, to a prior rule, to disclosure only, over four sessions — each refusal rule broken by names or grouping. Prefer **disclosure** where the theory says "the prior decides it anyway".
- Model checking must not be gated by the model. Striking the After-act when the model said it could not teach blinded E7 exactly where the model was wrong (J27).
- A conjugate update is Bayes only when each record's likelihood already has the family's form; when a local is summed out, fractional counts are a second learning mechanism and depend on order (CHARTER v0.2 appendix D).
- Diagnostics see only what records carry: a confounded misdeclaration and an act the policy stopped taking are invisible to every record-reading check (C27). Say what a check cannot see.
- The episode is an approximation of the whole run (§2 of v0.2), like the depth floor. Price every approximation and print it (E3, E7).

**About the process**
- Attackers find the author's errors far more often than the kernel's: most of what six charter sessions found was in pages and references I wrote. The builder's `QUESTIONS.md` is the same — read it as a review of the reference.
- Carry every fix through the whole page. Two sessions in a row found that the previous draft's fixes had not been propagated to C27, E7 or the appendices.
- Verify a PR against the kit as tagged, not your working tree.
- Check the attacker received the exact page: grep for a string only the current draft contains. Once, a half-applied edit reached the branch and was attacked instead of the intended text.

**About the tooling** (the owner works from steel since 2026-09-30, before that a laptop, and Android, with Claude Code)
- A file the owner is to use must be *presented* (the file card) or it cannot be downloaded; say so in the reply.
- Browsers save a second download as `name (1).ext`. Before each round, clear old copies from `~/Downloads`; after copying, `grep` the draft number before committing.
- Copying files into `master`'s working tree and then checking out a branch that tracks them fails; check out the branch first.
- Isolate attack sessions: a fresh directory outside `~/git`; no web (the repos are public and hold the reference); no user-level `CLAUDE.md` that mentions the project; deny reads outside the directory.
- Give commands in short, gated chains, each printing what it checked, with the expected output stated.

**About the owner**
- Rulings are the owner's. Recommend clearly, give the alternative in a line, and fill the table only after the owner answers.
- The owner values first principles, simplicity, and honesty over momentum: when you were wrong, say so plainly and fix it; when a step is optional, say what skipping it costs.
