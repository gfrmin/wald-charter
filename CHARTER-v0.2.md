# CHARTER v0.2 — amendment: what is learned between episodes

Name: **wald**. Status: **draft 9, unsigned.** In force from the author-signed tag `charter-v0.2`, together with `charter-v0` and `charter-v0.1`. Where the pages speak of the same thing, the later decides; everything this page does not mention stands as signed. Nothing earlier is edited (§8 of v0).

Marks **[J20]–[J26]** are author judgements, ruled in §10. Everything unmarked follows from §1 below and from §2 of v0 and v0.1. The reference is `laws/counts_check.py`.

Where the field has a word, this page uses it: a dimension shared across episodes is a **Global** (a population parameter of hierarchical Bayes); a dimension drawn afresh each episode is **local**; the run over which a Global is shared is a **plate**; an observation drawn into a later decision is an **information arc** (Lauritzen & Nilsson 2001). Earlier drafts coined `Latent`, `Carry` and `Cohort`; the coinages are withdrawn.

## 0. What forced this

**Two failures in credence**, this law's predecessor (github.com/gfrmin/credence, read 2026-09-22), both about the same missing thing. Its router learned each model's quality jointly with the reliability of the signal that graded it, from that signal alone, with no ground truth: the quality it most needed could not be told from its mirror image, and the code says so — a hand-set prior inequality was "load-bearing" to break the label symmetry, and the update was one expectation-maximisation step on soft counts. Its governor had ground truth, but the label discarded 49.3% of outcomes, so every evidence channel ran at 0.99 or above; on a grid capped at 0.9 the posterior converged, correctly, on a rung the true rate was not, and grew more certain with data. **One had no verdict; the other had a verdict that carried no information.**

**Three consumers of this law** have the same hole from the other side: an instrument's reliabilities hand-set while graded outcomes contradict them in a definite direction (of 31 decides where the channel said "gather", 28 were graded right and none wrong); a channel fitted once, offline, never moved; instruments calibrated by a script before play and frozen, so the most consequential number in the World is the one the law never sees. In each, the truth arrives **after** the act, it is about **the instrument** and not this question, and an episode of v0.1 has nowhere to put it.

**What did not force this.** Correlated instruments are already sayable: Ω as a product with a difficulty dimension, kernels that read the whole state, utilities that ignore it. **Drift is not made sayable here**, and draft 5 was wrong to say it already was: v0.2 has no transition of a Global between episodes, so a drift rate declared in Ω would change nothing across a plate. When drift is forced it will be said as a declared transition between episodes — content, not a forgetting factor; proplang measured that route matching an oracle-tuned forgetter (339.8 bits against 340.9) — and it will cost Counts their sufficiency (§4).

The numbers in this section measure other systems and are cited, not reproduced: the router's model is `src/routing.jl` of credence; the governor's are its `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-09-proplang-replan-post-19.md`; the forgetting-factor measurement is proplang's `design.md` §8. The appendices reproduce everything this page itself claims.

## 1. First principles, and where this page sits

**What a state is and what an act is.** There is one World, and its variables differ only in what the agent does with them. A variable the agent is uncertain about and cannot choose is **averaged over**; a variable it chooses is **maximised over**. That is the whole difference, and three consequences follow. *A past act is a fact*: it is data, and any likelihood may read it, which is why an after-act's kernel may read how the episode ended and why Counts may record which acts were taken, without acts becoming states. *Another agent's act would be a state*, which is why more than one agent is deferred rather than already sayable. *The agent's own future acts are resolved by maximisation, not belief*, which is why a floor is an approximation with a measured price (E3 of v0).

**The map.** The ambition is one small language in which the whole of Bayesian decision theory is sayable. wald supports a subset, and the subset is named so that what is missing is a list and not a surprise.

| the theory | wald |
| --- | --- |
| finite states, strictly positive prior; acts, utilities; observations, priced; conditioning; the Bayes act; sequential decisions and value of information | v0 |
| the cost of computing the decision | v0.1 (the think act) |
| parameters shared across episodes, learned from what arrives after the act | **v0.2, this page** |
| parameters that drift | §9, deferred — needs a declared transition of the Globals between episodes (§0) |
| acting in order to learn | §9, deferred — its price is measured (§2, E7) |
| hypotheses generated by a grammar, the prior as description length | §9, deferred |
| the verbs sayable inside a pack, so a program can reason about its own inference | §9, deferred — this is the gap between a calculator and an agent, and wald is still on the calculator's side of it |
| utility uncertain, elicited and revised | §9, deferred |
| continuous states; states not enumerated; more than one agent | §9, deferred |

wald trades the reflexivity of the sayable-verbs row for **auditability**: because a pack cannot compute, a stranger can judge every pack and every implementation against the kit. Each deferred row is a later amendment, forced by a measurement or not added at all.

## 2. The episode is a decomposition, and it is an approximation

The exact object over a plate of T episodes is **one World**: T episodes, each of horizon N, maximised as a single run. This page adopts instead the **episode decomposition**: maximise within an episode, never across. It is exact when the agent would never take an act for what it would teach, and that is false in general. On appendix B's World the decomposition costs nothing over two episodes, then 0.0393 at T = 3, 0.3499 at T = 5 and 0.8702 at T = 8 (exactly 8701945558695181/10¹⁶). **[J21]** takes it anyway, as E3 took the floor: a World that must be solved over its whole run is not one a consumer can declare a question at a time. E7 prints the price.

## 3. Nouns added

| noun | what it is | what dies without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global | a declared dimension of Ω that is a property of the World: an instrument's reliability, a model's quality. Each episode draws its locals afresh, independently of every other episode, from P(local \| Global): a law the Global governs. No utility and no ending utility reads a Global. A Global's values are names; the numbers a kernel reads under them are Kernel cells, sourced as any Kernel cell is — usually `elicited`, since which hypotheses to entertain is the owner's. In a World that declares a catch-all ⊥ (v0 S5), ⊥ is a local value — "this question's answer is none of the declared ones" — and P(⊥ \| Global) is declared under each Global. **[J20]** | nothing can persist; calibration stays outside the law (§0) |
| After-act | an act declared `after`, at most one per World, taken once the episode has **ended** — a terminal fired, or an ending outcome reached, whose end is the pair (act, outcome) — with kernel K_after(o \| ω, e) reading the state and the end e, declared for every end. It is not in the menu M; Price's domain is O and the After-act. It runs once per episode, so a grader's persistent bias must be declared as a Global. In a World that declares ⊥, K_after(o \| ⊥, e) > 0 for every o and e, as v0 S5 asks of every kernel that mints a report the World must absorb. | a reliability becomes unidentifiable whenever reports alone are symmetric in it (appendix A′); credence's router is this case |
| Counts | the multiset of the plate's **records**, one for every episode not ended in WORLD_FALSIFIED (under J26 only a plate's last episode can be): the sequence of draws taken — each (act, outcome), a `fresh` act taken twice being two draws — the end reached, and the after-report if one was taken. Facts, not beliefs. Source `data`. A **plate** is every episode run under one declaration of the pack. Counts are facts, not a model's: a new declaration — a refit, say — may start its plate from Counts another wrote, shipped by digest, provided its acts and outcomes can express every record, and it conditions on them under its own kernels. Two running plates never share Counts. **[J22] [J24]** | nothing learned can be held without a growing belief (proplang #25) |

## 4. Verbs — the added semantics

```
prior      the prior of an episode is   P(Global | Counts) · P(local | Global)
           where P(Global | Counts) ∝ P(Global) · Π_records L(record | Global)^count
           and   L(record | Global) = Σ_local P(local | Global) · Π_(k, o) K_k(o | local, Global)
                                                               · K_after(o_a | local, Global, e)   if an after-report was taken
end        every episode not ended in WORLD_FALSIFIED enters its record into Counts
after      once the episode has ended at e, before its record enters: if an After-act is declared and some
           after-outcome could move the Globals — its likelihood, given the episode's draws and end, differs between
           two Global values the belief at that moment keeps alive — the episode pays its price and the door reports
           o_a, which joins the record. If o_a has probability 0 under that belief, the World is falsified: the episode enters no
           record and the plate ends WORLD_FALSIFIED. [J26]
```

Nothing here is a new rule of inference: `prior` is the Bayes' theorem v0 §2's `condition` applies to one token, applied to each record's likelihood, and `end` and `after` are bookkeeping. The unit is the **record**, not the draw, because the draws within one episode share that episode's local state; within a record they are summed together over it. A record enters whether or not an after-act was taken: reports alone may inform a Global (appendix F), and C23 holds only if every episode's evidence is kept.

**Why Counts and not a posterior.** Both are exact and they agree (C23). A carried posterior's exact representation grows with every episode; Counts are sufficient for it — under the decomposition the episodes of a plate are independent given the Globals — and their support is bounded by the number of distinct records a World can produce; only their multiplicities grow with the plate, to at most R · ⌈log₂(T + 1)⌉ bits for R distinct records. Learning enters the law as a `data` table that grows, which S3 already governs, and order invariance (C22) is a property of a multiset.

**Conjugacy, and why Globals live on grids.** v0.2 keeps each Global on a finite grid, where the prior from Counts is Bayes' theorem itself and nothing is approximated. A conjugate family (a Beta over a reliability, say) is Bayes only when every record's likelihood already has the family's form in the Global — in practice, when the local is revealed. When a local is summed out the exact posterior is a mixture, and every shortcut to a single member of the family — fractional counts, moment matching, treating a noisy verdict as perfect, classifying each record's local and conditioning as if it were observed — is a second learning mechanism. Appendix D gives the numbers; `counts_check.py` carries the last of these as a poison.

**What drift would cost.** Counts are sufficient when the Globals are constant over the plate. A declared transition of the Globals between episodes (§9) would make episodes dependent given them; Counts would stop being sufficient, and the kernel would have to persist a belief, which S13 forbids until that amendment says otherwise.

## 5. Structural rules added

- **S11 A Global is unpaid.** No utility and no ending utility reads a Global dimension; refused by the name GLOBAL. No local is carried: each episode redraws its locals from the declared P(local \| Global); what moves is P(Global \| Counts), and with it the local's marginal — so a local that is a function of a Global, the answer to a question whose answer never changes, is learned lawfully. The rule is one of form, not of meaning: a World refused GLOBAL has a lawful twin whose utility reads a local that copies the Global, with the same beliefs and acts. What S11 buys is that a pack keeps what persists and what pays in separate tables, so a reader and the kit can tell them apart.
- **S12 The after-act is after.** Taken only once the episode has ended; it changes no earned utility, and it adds no value to any act: an act's value is its expected utility over the episode, as if no after-report were coming (J21). An implementation that computes the same expectation through the after-act's kernel, by total probability, is lawful; one that credits an act with what its after-report would teach is not. Struck, and its price not paid, when no after-outcome could move the Globals (§4) — which covers every Global being a point mass, and every spread that lies wholly within a class S15 cannot separate. An abstention is an end like any other and is graded. A pack that declares a second after-act, whose after-act kernel omits an end or reads anything but the state and the end, or which declares ⊥ and gives some after-outcome probability 0 at ⊥, is refused by the name AFTER.
- **S13 Counts are data, content-addressed.** Written only by the kernel from what the door reported. Counts join v0.1 S3's list of tables, so their multiplicities are housed numerals. A pack that ships Counts declares their sha-256 in the canonical encoding SURFACE v0.2 is to give; the digest is checked once, at declaration, and Counts that do not hash to it are refused by the name PLATE. The digest is a name, not a numeral. Nothing else persists between episodes: no log, no cache with a meaning. That last clause is judged structurally, like S6–S10 of v0.1: a kernel that keeps a log beside its Counts, persists the posterior they determine, or carries the end-of-episode joint belief, gives the reference's prior at every episode (attack session 1, K1–K3), and only the structural kit and the author's reading can see it.
- **S14 A learned Prior is scored.** A pack that ships Counts ships the held-out score of the Prior they condition: the **leave-one-out predictive probability** of the shipped records — for each copy of each record, P(its outcomes \| its acts) under the Prior conditioned on all the others, multiplied together. It is a rational, it needs no split, and the kernel recomputes it at declaration: a pack that ships none, or one that differs, is refused by the name UNSCORED. Its logarithm is a display, not a table. Counts the kernel writes during a plate are scored by E7, not re-declared. **[J23]**
- **S15 A Global must be learnable, and the part of the prior no plate will move is shown.** A **realisable design** is a sequence of draws within the horizon N, each `once` act at most once, then an end, then the After-act. Two values of the Globals are **inseparable** if every realisable design gives every record the same probability under both; since a distinguishing adaptive policy — and the kernel's is deterministic — implies a distinguishing fixed design, inseparable values are exactly those no policy and no plate can ever tell apart. They fall into classes. A pack that declares a Global is refused by the name UNIDENTIFIED if there is only one class: nothing can be learned, and the Global is a local that pretends to persist. Otherwise it is accepted, and the kernel discloses at declaration every class whose members disagree about the local jointly with the records: within such a class the declared prior settles, forever, something a utility reads, and no plate will move it. That is what a prior does; wald's rule is not that it be absent but that it be **declared, sourced and shown**. Values with the same joint law are twins — one hypothesis spelt twice — and are not listed. The rule reads no names: splitting a value into twins neither creates nor removes a separating design, nor changes what is disclosed (appendix I). **[J25]**

## 6. Consequences — theorems of §4, tests in `laws/counts_check.py`

- **C21 Inert without a Global.** In a World that declares no Global, every episode it runs plays as v0.1.
- **C22 Order invariance.** The prior of episode n+1 depends on the first n only through Counts.
- **C23 Sufficiency.** That prior's Global marginal equals episode-by-episode v0 conditioning on the full product Ω, locals redrawn each episode.
- **C24 No leakage.** The prior formed from given Counts reads no utility, price or rate. What Counts come to contain does: the policy chooses which draws are taken and which end is reached, and the policy reads utilities and prices — appendix B's agent never asks B because of them.
- **C25 Expected utility, not the chance of being best.** Where "the chance an act is best" and expected utility disagree, the act is expected utility's. (proplang #24 was the other rule, making declared utilities silently inert.)
- **C26 Not a theorem.** Learning does not raise the policy value, and the decomposition does not attain the plate's value.
- **C27 The railed rung, and the unconverged posterior.** Under a fixed design, a Global on a grid concentrates on the set of grid points minimising the KL divergence from the truth's record distribution to the model's, for that design — and if every point's divergence is infinite, the plate ends WORLD_FALSIFIED; where that set is one point it may lie on the other side of a decision threshold from the truth, and more data makes the agent more certain, not more right; where it has several points the posterior need not converge within that set. Under the agent's own adaptive design a misdeclared Global need not converge either: the act that produces data can depend on the belief the data would move, and the posterior can keep crossing a threshold (appendix E). E7 catches both **in the acts the policy takes**. A misdeclaration in an act the policy has stopped taking is invisible to every check that reads records, E7 and the held-out score included (appendix G): only acting to learn would see it, which is the price of J21.
- C13 and C14 of v0.1 (gauge, sure-thing) hold episode by episode with Counts in force; C14's h(ω) reads no Global.

## 7. Evaluators added

- **E7 What was learned, and what it cost.** At declaration the scoreboard prints S15's disclosure: every class of inseparable values whose members disagree about the local, with its declared prior mass. Over a plate it prints: the policy value with Counts and with the declared Prior alone, each net of every price paid, the After-act's included (the declared-Prior agent takes no After-act: it learns nothing); the value of the same World maximised as one run, from the exact solver in `laws/` (d = N, free to decline the After-act), where the kit's size bound admits it, and the difference (§2's price), over plate lengths the kit sweeps — a plate's length and that size bound are the kit's, not numbers in any pack; the held-out score of the learned Prior; and, always, for **every draw** in Counts — each report and each after-report — grouped by the history within its episode that led to it, the **total variation distance** between the empirical distribution of its outcome and its posterior predictive given that history, under P(Global \| all Counts), the scored draws included. Because the choice to take a draw depends only on what came before it, that predictive carries no selection, and when the World is right the line shrinks toward zero. When a label discards negatives or a grid cannot hold the true rate, it does not: on appendix C's degenerate label it prints 0.09 at every plate length, the governor's own \|p1 − rate\|; on appendix E's adaptive plate, 3/10. It costs no observation and no price.

## 8. Residues

The assumption that a Global is constant over its plate; a misdeclaration in an act the policy has stopped taking, which no record reveals (C27, appendix G); the part of the prior no plate will move, which S15 discloses; the plate's boundary, which the owner declares and the kernel does not discover; the decomposition of §2, whose price E7 prints; and the definition of each after-act's outcome — what counts as a success is the owner's, and credence's governor shows that one sentence of it can empty a channel.

v0.1's "a `fitted` f is learned across episodes (J7)", and the same of Cost, means refitted between packs, not within a plate: within a plate nothing but Counts persists.

## 9. Deferred, each its own amendment

**Drift** — a declared transition of the Globals between episodes; content, not a forgetting factor. **Acting to learn** — the decomposition made optional; needs a tractable form first. **Generated hypotheses** — Ω by a grammar, the prior as description length; proplang's model fragment is the prototype, and the forcing measurement is already visible in two consumers (unlisted children in one, a NONE in another). **Sayable verbs** — reflexive closure, the calculator's last organ; a candidate forcing measurement is v0.1's think act, inert on three Worlds, where proplang's clock-terminated deliberation was not. **Sharing across Worlds** — one instrument's reliability used by every World that calls it. **Utility as a Global** — forbidden today by S11. **Continuous carriers** — a continuous Global may use a conjugate family only under §4's rule, and otherwise must carry the exact mixture or be refused. **Open Ω**, **more than one agent**.

## Appendix A — what one graded episode is worth

Every World in these appendices is `closed`; Appendix D is an illustration, not a World.

Ω = {a1, a2} × {good, poor}: the answer (local) and the instrument's reliability (Global). Declared: the Global uniform; P(answer \| Global) uniform. One `once` act `ask`, free: it reports the answer with probability 9/10 when good and 3/5 when poor, else the other. Terminals in order `say a1`, `say a2`, `abstain`: +1 right, −2 wrong, 0. N = 1, d = 1. One After-act: free, perfect, reveals the answer.

- **Episode 1.** Report `a1`; P(a1 \| report a1) = 3/4; `say a1` at 1/4.
- **The after-report** says a1. Counts hold one record. P(Global \| Counts) = **(3/5, 2/5)**; had it said a2, **(1/5, 4/5)**.
- **Episode 2.** The answer starts uniform again. The same report gives P(a1) = **39/50** and `say a1` at **17/50**, against 3/4 and 1/4 without Counts.
- **C22.** One right then one wrong gives **(3/11, 8/11)**, and so would the reverse — as arithmetic. As a plate the reverse never happens: after a wrong first grade the Global is at (1/5, 4/5), E[reliability] = 33/50 < 2/3, and the kernel abstains without asking, forever. Under the declared model it is still asking at episode 60 with probability about 0.4033; with a good instrument, about 19.4% of plates have stopped learning by then after an early wrong grade, giving up 7/10 an episode. That is §2's price on this page's headline World.
- **A′, the deletion proof of the after-act.** The same World with no After-act is refused UNIDENTIFIED: P(report = a1) = 1/2 whatever the reliability, so no count of reports can ever move it.

## Appendix C — credence's two failures, as Worlds

- **The router (R).** A turn is correct (local) with probability θ, the model's quality (Global); the exec signal, one `once` free act, fires with ρ if correct and σ if not (Globals); θ, ρ, σ ∈ {1/4, 3/4}, θ uniform. Terminals `use` (+1 correct, −1 not) and `skip` (0); N = 1, d = 1. With no after-act, (θ, ρ, σ) and (1 − θ, σ, ρ) are inseparable — four classes, separated from one another by P(fire) ∈ {1/4, 3/8, 5/8, 3/4} — and within each, "is this model good?" is exactly what the two members disagree on. ## Appendix D — why a conjugate shortcut needs the local revealed

An illustration outside wald's semantics: the prior here is continuous (§9, continuous carriers).

θ is the probability an instrument reports the truth, with a uniform prior. A verdict reveals the truth with probability 4/5, so each record says only whether verdict and report agreed: P(agree | θ) = 1/5 + 3/5·θ. Seven agree, three disagree.

- **Bayes.** The posterior is a polynomial of degree ten — a mixture of eleven Betas — with mean **654561/909967 ≈ 0.7193**.
- **Treating the verdict as perfect.** Beta(8, 4), mean 2/3 ≈ 0.6667: biased.
- **Fractional counts** (add the E-step's π and 1 − π to a Beta, record by record): ≈ 0.7192 when the agreements come first, ≈ 0.5526 when the disagreements do. The same ten records, two answers: C22 fails, which is how a second learning mechanism shows itself.

With a perfect verdict each record is θ or 1 − θ, the Beta is exact, and the grid and the conjugate family agree.

## Appendix E — the held threshold (attack session 1, F11)

Global g ∈ {x, y}, uniform. The local is a pair of success bits (s_A, s_B), independent given g: s_A is 1 with 1/2 under x and 9/10 under y; s_B the reverse. Terminals `useA`, `useB`: +1 if the used instrument succeeds, −1 if not. No observational acts. A free After-act reveals the bit of the terminal fired. N = 1, d = 1. Truth off the grid: each bit is 1 with 2/5.

- **Fixed design** (always `useA`): 100 records at 2/5 put P(x) at 1 less a sliver; E7 prints 1/10 plus 2/5 of that sliver, 0.1000 — C27's fixed-design case.
- **The agent's design**: it plays `useA` exactly when P(x) ≤ 1/2, so A-data arrive when the belief favours y and push it toward x, and B-data the reverse. Equal Counts of each at 2/5 hold the posterior at **(1/2, 1/2)**; E7 prints **3/10** for each draw. It does not converge, but it is not pinned either: over all paths max(P(x), P(y)) is at least 99/100 at T = 200 with probability about 0.0029, and reaches 99/100 at some time up to T = 200 with probability about 0.22 (0.2211 by t = 199, 0.2222 by t = 200) (both from attack session 2's exact enumeration) — one failure moves the odds fivefold.

## Appendix G — what no record can show (attack session 2, 2.1)

Global g ∈ {x, y}, uniform. The local is two success bits: s_A is 1 with 7/10 under x and 3/10 under y; s_B with 1/2 under both. Terminals `useA`, `useB` at ±1 on their bit; a free After-act reveals the bit of the terminal fired; no observational acts; N = 1, d = 1. The kernel plays `useA` iff P(x) ≥ 1/2. The truth: s_A with 7/10, s_B with **9/10** — so `useB` is worth 4/5 and `useA` 2/5, and the misdeclaration is in B.

- A-records at 7/10 fit x exactly: the posterior goes to x, the agent stays on `useA`, and E7 prints 0 — correctly, for every record it has.
- B-records carry no information about the Global, so once A-failures outnumber A-successes the agent moves to `useB` and stays. Over its own plate it rails on `useA` with probability 4/7 (gambler's ruin at ratio 3/7), forgoing 2/5 an episode, with E7 silent throughout; on the other branch E7 prints 2/5 for B.
- The error lives in an act the policy has stopped taking. No check that reads records can see it; acting to learn could. This is conceded, and it is the same fact as §2's price.
